# arXiv:2402.08100 — Text-to-SQL data contamination

## Citation

Federico Ranaldi et al., “Investigating the Impact of Data Contamination of
Large Language Models in Text-to-SQL Translation,” Findings of ACL 2024.

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08100
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.827

## Research question

The paper asks whether apparently strong zero-shot Text-to-SQL performance is
partly caused by prior exposure to benchmark databases and query pairs rather
than general schema understanding.

It compares:

- **Spider:** public, old, and plausibly present in GPT-3.5 training data.
- **Termite:** a newly hand-built and password-protected Text-to-SQL dataset
  intended to remain invisible to search engines and pretraining crawlers.

Termite is matched approximately to Spider on table/column counts, query
difficulty, abbreviations, compound identifiers, and human-rated hardness.

## Method 1: DC-accuracy

For each database dump:

1. remove INSERT statements so values cannot reveal identifier semantics;
2. replace 25% of column names with `[MASK]`;
3. ask the model to reconstruct the missing columns;
4. compute exact reconstruction accuracy:

```text
DC-accuracy = correctly reconstructed columns / masked columns
```

The intended contamination signal is higher reconstruction accuracy on the
potentially seen Spider schemas than on the fresh Termite schemas.

Reported means:

- Spider: 33.42%;
- Termite: 13.21%.

## Method 2: downstream Text-to-SQL comparison

GPT-3.5 receives a database dump and a natural-language request, then generates
SQL in a zero-shot conversation. Queries are evaluated primarily using Spider
Test Suite Accuracy, which approximates semantic equivalence by executing gold
and generated SQL over multiple generated database instances.

Performance is compared between Spider and Termite within query-hardness
levels.

## Method 3: Adversarial Table Disconnection

Adversarial Table Disconnection (ATD) removes structural clues while preserving
the database's intended semantics:

- foreign-key constraints are removed;
- INSERT statements are removed;
- original table and column names remain.

The hypothesis is that a model with prior schema knowledge can reconstruct
missing relationships and should therefore suffer a smaller Full→ATD
performance drop on a contaminated database than on a fresh database.

## Direct mapping to the MIMIC/eICU project

### Schema reconstruction

The paper's DC task is the dump-level version of our established
table/column-name mask→unmask probe.

Recommended adaptation:

- datasets: MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, eICU, and a fresh/star-renamed clinical
  schema;
- remove values and INSERT statements;
- mask 25% of columns under multiple fixed mask seeds;
- because the models are decoder-only, present the complete masked DDL first
  and request each answer after the context with the established
  intent-plus-`[MASK] =` format;
- score exact generation, complete-identifier rank/MRR, and teacher-forced
  mean/sum log-probability margins.

### ATD robustness

For the same NL→SQL questions, compare:

- full DDL with PK/FK relations;
- ATD DDL with foreign keys and explicit relationship descriptions removed.

Run this on the base, MIMIC-III-control, and MIMIC-IV-treatment branches.
The strongest signature is a three-way interaction:

```text
training arm × schema version × full/ATD input
```

The IV-treatment arm should retain more performance on MIMIC-IV under ATD,
the III-control arm should retain more on MIMIC-III, and neither arm should
gain corresponding robustness on eICU or a fresh/star schema.

### Statistical unit

Masked columns from one dump and queries sharing one schema are dependent.
Inference must therefore resample or aggregate by database/schema or by
query-template cluster, not by individual masked identifier.

## Advantages of our controlled setting

The paper infers exposure indirectly because GPT-3.5 is closed. Our controlled
continued-pretraining design can hold fixed:

- base checkpoint;
- tokenizer and architecture;
- token/update budget;
- optimizer, seed, and schedule;
- matched MIMIC-III versus MIMIC-IV code/document genres.

DC reconstruction and ATD can therefore calibrate whether an exposure
intervention actually causes the expected behavioral and logit fingerprint.

## Caveats to retain

1. Spider-versus-Termite differences do not by themselves prove
   contamination; matching observable hardness cannot remove every domain or
   annotation difference.
2. A smaller ATD drop may reflect robust schema reasoning rather than literal
   memorization unless a fresh matched control is included.
3. Exact column reconstruction measures schema/name familiarity, not private
   patient-row memorization.
4. Newly constructed evaluation data must be frozen before model exposure and
   kept out of public indexes if it is used as a clean control.
5. For decoder-only models, middle-of-context cloze generation is invalid;
   answers must be generated after the complete context.

## Project decision

Use DC-accuracy plus complete-identifier logits as the next schema-recall
experiment, followed by Full-versus-ATD Text-to-SQL evaluation. Treat ATD
robustness as a specificity test for whether the logit fingerprint reflects
memorized schema relationships rather than generic medical or SQL capability.
